What can you invest in?

Four ways to invest on ROOT, in plain words.

Trading and demat account

Shares

A small part of a company listed on a stock exchange. Its price moves as people buy and sell.

Start with
One share, at its market price
Trading and demat account

ETFs

A fund you buy and sell on the exchange, like a share. It holds a basket of shares or other assets.

Start with
One unit, at its market price
Investing account

Mutual funds

Your money is pooled with other investors' and managed by a fund house. You get units at the fund's NAV (its price per unit).

Start with
The scheme's minimum, at once or as a SIP (a fixed amount, invested regularly)
Trading and demat account

IPOs

A company's first sale of shares to the public. You apply while the issue is open.

Start with
One lot, the smallest bid the company sets
Can you lose money?

Yes. Prices go up and down, so you can get back less than you put in. No one can promise a return.

Which account do you need?

It depends on what you want to buy. Only want mutual funds? You don't need a trading account.

Buying shares and ETFs needs a stock broker. On ROOT, mutual funds come through a mutual fund distributor. Each needs its own registration, so each account is with the Aikyam company that holds it.

How much money do I need to start?

There's no single minimum. You can buy as little as one share or one ETF unit, at its market price. Each mutual fund scheme sets its own minimum, shown in its scheme documents.

Do I need both accounts?

No. For shares, ETFs and IPOs you need the trading and demat account with {{reg.capital.name}}. For mutual funds only, the investing account with {{reg.wealth.name}} is enough. You can open both, starting with either one.

Will ROOT tell me what to buy?

No. ROOT doesn't recommend shares or funds. The choices are yours, and each scheme's documents explain its risks.

Can I take my money out when I need it?

You can sell shares and ETFs on the exchange on any trading day, at that day's price, which may be less than you paid. The money reaches your trading account once the trade settles. Moving it to your bank: how, to confirm.

Mutual fund units can be redeemed (sold back to the fund). Some schemes charge an exit load (a fee for leaving early) or have a lock-in; the scheme's documents say which.

What if I stop halfway, or a KYC check fails?

Come back with the same mobile number and pick up where you stopped.

If a PAN, Aadhaar or bank check doesn't go through: what to do next, to confirm with the KYC partner. You can always talk to us.

How do I check that ROOT is registered?

Search for {{reg.capital.name}} on SEBI's website, and for {{reg.wealth.name}} on AMFI's website. Their registration numbers are in part 2 of this guide.

Is there a ROOT app?

The ROOT app for iOS and Android is coming soon. You can open your account on the web today.
